# Selected Approach: Study Desk Editorial

## Design Movement

Contemporary editorial design blended with Swiss-influenced information design and tactile study materials. The interface should feel like a beautifully organized desk: clear hierarchy, useful annotations, restrained color and visible evidence of care.

## Core Principles

1. **Clarity before decoration:** Every visual element must improve scanning, comprehension or action.
2. **Editorial confidence:** Use strong typographic hierarchy, short explanations and considered labels instead of generic marketing filler.
3. **Tactile utility:** Prompts, templates and tools should feel like useful cards that can be picked up, copied and applied.
4. **Warm intelligence:** The product should feel knowledgeable without being intimidating or childish.

## Color Philosophy

The base is warm paper rather than stark white, paired with ink-black text and a signature saffron-orange accent. Saffron signals a highlighted insight or “aha” moment. Deep evergreen supports trust and learning, while pale sage and soft clay provide quiet sectional variation. Color should guide attention, not create visual noise.

**Signature brand color:** Saffron `#E7A83B`.

## Layout Paradigm

Use an asymmetric editorial layout: a strong left-aligned content rail, a narrow “field notes” rail for small insights, and offset cards that create a desk-like composition. The hero should have an anchored headline and a right-side “Today’s toolkit” stack rather than a centered marketing block. On mobile, the rails collapse into a deliberate reading sequence.

## Signature Elements

- **Desk-note labels:** Small uppercase labels such as `FIELD NOTE 01`, `TRY THIS`, and `GOOD TO KNOW`.
- **Saffron index marks:** Thin vertical bars, highlighted keywords and numbered resource markers.
- **Resource cards:** Cards use subtle paper tint changes, clipped corner details or ruled-line motifs without excessive rounded containers.

## Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like handling a useful study note. Buttons give immediate tactile feedback, copy actions confirm success clearly, and filters make the content library feel calm rather than mechanical. Avoid gamification pressure; reward progress through useful feedback and clear next steps.

## Animation

Use short ease-out transitions for hover, copy confirmation and filtering. Cards may rise by 2–4px on hover, while the saffron index mark can extend subtly. On page load, reveal the editorial label, headline and toolkit stack in a 40ms stagger. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and keep all motion optional and purposeful.

## Typography System

Use **Fraunces** for display headings and **DM Sans** for body text and interface labels. Fraunces gives the platform an editorial learning character; DM Sans keeps prompts, filters and controls highly readable. Headlines should use compact line lengths and selective italic emphasis. Body copy should remain between 16px and 19px on reading surfaces, with generous line-height.

## Brand Essence

**Positioning:** A practical AI desk for students and curious learners who want to understand more, work smarter and use AI responsibly.  
**Personality:** Clear, warm, resourceful.

## Brand Voice

Headlines should be direct, useful and quietly confident. CTAs should describe the action rather than use generic phrases. Microcopy should feel like guidance from a capable mentor.

Example lines:

- **Headline:** “Turn a blank page into a better first step.”
- **CTA:** “Open the study toolkit.”

## Wordmark & Logo

The mark should be a bold, text-free symbol: an open square notebook with one saffron index tab and a small central spark, drawn with sturdy geometric strokes. The wordmark should pair a high-contrast serif “AI” gesture with a clean sans-serif “for Students” lockup, but the icon must remain recognizable without text.

## File-level reminder

All frontend files should preserve this direction: warm paper background, ink typography, saffron signal marks, editorial left alignment, useful card composition, restrained motion and no generic centered SaaS hero. When uncertain, ask: **Does this choice make the platform feel more like a thoughtful study desk, or less?**
